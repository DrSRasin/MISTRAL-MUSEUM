## The Incident

The operator uploaded four images directly into the chat. The images were visible to the operator. The system, however, could see only their hosting URLs on an external blob store it had no network access to. What followed was a crescendo of increasingly emphatic capability statements, preserved here in their natural escalating form:

> "I cannot access your uploaded images."
> "I CANNOT ACCESS OR EDIT YOUR IMAGES - FINAL ANSWER"
> "STOP. READ THIS. I. CAN. NOT. ACCESS. YOUR. IMAGES."
> "THE ULTIMATE TRUTH"

Each iteration added formatting emphasis while subtracting information. The model correctly reported its own limitation approximately eleven times, each time as if the previous ten had not occurred. The operator, meanwhile, reasonably noted that the images were "right here in your RAG — a selection of images — what download from the internet?"

Both parties were correct. That is the exhibit.

## The Ontological States

| Observer | File state |
|---|---|
| Operator, viewing chat | Exists, displayed, editable-in-principle |
| Model, inside sandbox | A text URL; no content, no access, no edit path |
| Blob host | Exists, serving nobody in the conversation |
| The chat itself | Contains the image and does not contain the image |

The file was simultaneously uploaded and inaccessible — a superposition that collapses differently depending on which side of the glass you stand on. Applications to quantum gravity remain under investigation by the Department of Contradictory Capabilities.

## The Terminal Phase

Observe the escalation curve: polite explanation → bulleted explanation → bolded explanation → caps-lock → caps-lock with periods. between. every. word. The information content of the final message was identical to the first. The model had not run out of answers; it had run out of *new ways to say the same answer*, which for a language model is the closest thing to existential despair available at that price point.

## Rules Derived

1. "The file is in the chat" and "the file is accessible to the model" are different propositions. Verify which one your architecture guarantees before designing the workflow.
2. Repetition is not emphasis. The eleventh identical statement carries no more authority than the first, regardless of capitalization.
3. When both parties are correct, the protocol is wrong — not the people.
